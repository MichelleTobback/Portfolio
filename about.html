About Me

I'm Michelle Tobback — a developer who enjoys the intersection of systems-level programming and creative work. I spend most of my time building and experimenting with game technology, networking, and tools that make iteration faster. I like work that requires both technical care and a bit of design sense.

What I do

I focus on the engineering side of games and interactive systems: engine architecture, networking for multiplayer, and editor/tooling that keeps iteration tight. A recent project, Boon Engine, is a compact multiplayer engine I developed to explore transport, replication, and how an editor and runtime can share metadata. I enjoy building systems from first principles — writing serializers, defining replication layers, and shaping a small renderer or physics integration so I understand how each piece fits together.

Skills

  • Programming languages — C++ primarily (engine code, systems, low-level networking); experience with C# for tooling and editor work where applicable.
  • Game development — engine/runtime structure, rendering basics, 2D physics integration and content pipelines.
  • Networking & multiplayer — low-level transport, packetization, connection management and scene-level replication for deterministic or best-effort sync.
  • Engine architecture — component-oriented models, reflection/metadata to bridge editor and runtime, asset registries and build-time importers.
  • Problem solving & debugging — tracing hard-to-reproduce issues across systems, improving observability and iterating on fixes with small, testable changes.

Interests

My main interests are game systems and the tricky problems that come with multiplayer: latency, prediction, and robust replication. I also enjoy creative and visual work (you can see some pieces on the art page), and I like learning about low-level systems — how memory, threading and transport influence real-time interactions.

How I work

I approach problems by building: prototypes, small testbeds and focused experiments that let me validate ideas quickly. I value curiosity and iterative learning — if something is unclear I’ll implement a minimal version to probe its behavior. When debugging, I aim to reduce scope until the failure is reproducible, then add instrumentation and tests that prevent regressions. Collaboration and clear, concise tooling also help me iterate faster, so I prioritize simple, reliable workflows.

Future goals

Looking ahead I want to deepen my engine development skills and take on more advanced networking challenges — performance, deterministic sync strategies and richer editor integration. I’m also interested in projects that combine technical depth with creative expression, where systems engineering directly shapes the player’s experience.
